WORTH A CONVERSATION

Visits per family fell sharply in 2025–26

Families served grew from 27 to 34, but total personal visits dropped from 368 to 144 — average visits per family fell from roughly 13–14 to about 4. One likely factor: 2025–26 also had the most staff turnover of any year, with 3 new hires and 2 departures on a team of about 5. New parent educators take time to build a full caseload, which would show up exactly like this. Worth confirming with the team either way.

WORTH CELEBRATING

The highest-need families are getting fully served

Families with two or more risk factors — substance use, housing instability, mental health concerns, and similar — hit their required 75%+ visit threshold at 100% in three of the last four years, matching or beating families with fewer risk factors every single year. Resource connections have also stayed strong throughout, with 94–100% of families connected to at least one community resource annually. The families who need the most are not falling through the cracks.

Families & children served

Both dropped sharply after 2022–23 and have been climbing back since — 2025–26 is nearly back to the original level.

Service volume

Total personal visits, and visits per family served — the second view is what shows the 2025–26 dip most clearly.

Staffing & turnover

All parent educator roles have been part-time across all four years — no full-time PEs in any year reported. 2025–26's turnover (3 hired, 2 departed) is the highest of the period.

Are the highest-need families getting enough visits?

Share of families hitting at least 75% of their required visits, split by risk-factor load. High-need families (2+ risk factors like substance use, housing instability, or mental health concerns) have matched or beaten lower-need families every year.

Why families leave, and how long they stay

2025–26 had the most exits (17) but also the most families who stayed 24+ months before leaving (9) and the most who left because a child aged out or graduated (5) — a sign of long, complete engagements, not just churn.

Goal achievement

Every family with at least one visit has had a goal documented, every year, without exception. But the share who go on to actually meet a goal has drifted down — from about 44% in 2023–24 to roughly 29–30% the last two years. Worth a closer look at what's changed in goal-setting or follow-through.

Resource connections

The share of families connected to at least one community resource — food assistance, housing support, medical referrals, and similar — has stayed consistently strong across all four years.

Community engagementnew — review me

Group connections (classes, playgroups, community events) are a second touchpoint beyond home visits. Attendance has drifted down since 2022–23 even as families served has recovered — worth watching alongside the visits-per-family trend.

Caseload per staff membernew — review me

Families served divided by total part-time-equivalent parent educators. A useful staffing-capacity check alongside the turnover chart above — 2025–26's larger team is actually carrying a lighter average caseload than prior years, which fits the "new staff ramping up" read on the visit dip.

Documentation compliancenew — review me

Family-Centered Assessments are a required piece of documentation, due within 120 days of enrollment (initial) and annually thereafter. Both have been completed for 100% of eligible families in every single year — a genuinely strong, tellable compliance record.

Monthly tracking — getting startednew — review me

The APR is only produced once a year, so it can't power a monthly view on its own. Everything above will stay an annual lookback unless we start capturing a few numbers more often. Here's a starting proposal — trim or expand it once you see what's realistic to gather.

What's realistic to track monthly (all of these can be pulled from a custom date-range report in Visit Tracker — no new system needed):

MetricWhy it's a good monthly signal
Families served this monthEarliest sign of a caseload change — don't wait a year to notice.
Visits delivered this monthThe core leading indicator behind this year's biggest concern.
New enrollmentsShows whether outreach/referrals are keeping pace.
Exits, and whyCatches a "no longer wants services" spike within weeks, not a year later.
Visits per family-in-caseloadThe dosage metric — catches a dip in real time instead of in next year's APR.

What to leave annual: immunization rate, goal-met rate, FCA compliance, and anything else that's inherently a full-year calculation — trying to track these monthly would mostly add noise.
